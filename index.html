02 — About

Five years
in, still early.

I'm Ben. I started designing in 2021 and I've been the Graphic Designer at The Canadian Brewhouse since 2026. Before that I spent two years at KMS Tools & Equipment on flyers, packaging and email, and my degree is a BFA in Graphic and Digital Design from the University of the Fraser Valley.

I played golf at UFV for seven years and captained the team for three. That's where most of what I know about deadlines comes from. You practise the same shot until it holds up under pressure, then you go play. Design works the same way. I hold five hospitality brands at once, build campaigns for the Super Bowl, the Stanley Cup Playoffs and UFC nights, and prepare press-ready files for wine label dielines, takeout boxes and in store signage. I'd rather build one system that survives a rushed Friday than five that only look good in a deck.

Packaging 2025

Close to Home

A three-bottle range — a white, a rosé and a red — drawn as one continuous landscape and split across three labels.

Client
The Swirl Wine Imports, Edmonton
Scope
Close to Home three-bottle range — landscape illustration, front, back and neck labels for Prima Luce, Ricordi and Ville Natale
Duration
2025

Three views from one place

The story behind these labels sit close to home for me. Looking back at the label design I created, that panoramic illustration of the Fraser River, the dogwood tree, and the Golden Ears mountains is the exact visual embodiment of a “sweet memory.”

The soft pink sunset wash in the background visually communicates the Rosé, while the new name perfectly grounds the deeply personal story of growing up in Langley, walking your dog, and looking out from the family porch. Each label has a meaning for me.

Campaign & print 2026

The Canadian Brewhouse

A national sports-bar group running a different reason to come in every week — concerts, playoffs, patio season — and needing all of it to look like one house.

Client
The Canadian Brewhouse
Scope
2026 Spring/Summer campaigns — Brew Bus concert series, playoff and league promotions, exclusive app deal digital set, patio and giveaway digital.
Duration
Ongoing, 2026

One house, every marquee

Each piece borrows the language of what it is selling — stadium light for the playoffs, stage smoke and torn tape for the Brew Bus, prairie green for the Roughriders — while the CBH lockup, the condensed type and the red rule stay exactly where guests expect them. The campaign changes voice without changing hands.

The same frame runs from an 11 × 17 in a snap frame to a 1080 × 1920 story to a letter-size sheet on a festival table, so a season of shows, games and specials can be produced week to week without redrawing the system each time.

Campaign & print 2024

The Banquet

A bowling-and-dining venue running a new promotion every day of the week, needing print and digital that could be swapped weekly without a redesign each time.

Client
The Banquet
Scope
2026 Spring/Summer campaigns — daily-specials poster series, Summer Strikes bowling pair, double-sided feature menu, Make-A-Wish digital set
Duration
Ongoing, 2026

One season, one system

The 2026 Spring/Summer campaign runs across print and screen from one set of rules: a fixed frame, a single type hierarchy, and photography treated the same way every time. Daily specials, Summer Strikes and the feature menu all sit inside it, so a guest walking from the door to the lanes reads one voice rather than a wall of unrelated bills.

The same system stretches to the Make-A-Wish digital set, where the frame becomes a four-card story cut for 1080 × 1920. Because the structure holds, the venue’s own team can swap a dish, a date or a price in an afternoon without art direction.

Brand identity 2025

Young Par

A junior golf brand for kids who are just starting out — built on a cast of characters rather than a crest, so the clubhouse feels like somewhere they belong.

Client
Young Par
Scope
Brand identity — wordmark and YP monogram, a cast of nine characters, apparel and headwear, stickers and course accessories
Duration
Ongoing, 2025

A cast, not a crest

Most golf brands lead with a crest and an old-course palette, which reads as somebody else’s club. Young Par leads with characters instead — a fox, a turtle, a kangaroo, a squirrel and five more, each drawn to the same rules: one heavy teal outline, flat fills, a carry bag over the shoulder and a club in hand.

The cast does the work a logo can’t. A kid picks a character, and the character carries the brand onto a hoodie, a bucket hat, a sticker sheet or a towel without the wordmark having to shout. Teal, orange and cream hold everything together across print, embroidery and merch.
